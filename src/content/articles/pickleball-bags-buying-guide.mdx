## Count the load before you pick a style

Three checks, then a shape.

1. **Paddles you actually bring.** A match paddle plus a backup is two. A demo set or a family session is more. Use the paddle count the listing prints.
2. **Shoes.** If you drive in street shoes and change at the court, you want a shoe compartment. If you walk in already wearing [court shoes](/guides/best-pickleball-shoes-2026), a sling can be enough.
3. **Outdoor balls.** A 40-hole ball is the usual outdoor ball. The [balls guide](/guides/pickleball-balls-buying-guide) separates indoor and outdoor packs. Only the Franklin sling on this page prints a ball count.

A gym duffel will move the gear. It will not keep a gritty paddle face off a wet shoe, and it will not hang on the fence between games.

## Sling bag

Choose a sling when the walk from the car is short, you play one session, and the shoes stay on your feet. You give up a real shoe tunnel. You gain a bag that does not need a clothing size, which is why a sling is the only bag shape that shows up as a step-up on the [stocking stuffers guide](/guides/best-pickleball-stocking-stuffers). This page does not re-rank grips, tape, or ball packs. If the gift has to fit a stocking, use that guide.

**Franklin Sports Pickleball Sling Bag ($).** Franklin Sports calls it the official sling of the US Open. The listing prints six paddles, six X-Performance 40 balls, a felt-lined pocket for a phone, room for a sports bottle, a carry strap, and an easy-to-use fence hook. Six balls is half of a typical outdoor 12-pack, not a full dozen.

**Mangrove Pickleball Sling Bag ($$).** The catalog description calls it a value sling with a fence hook. The feature lines print a reversible strap, a felt-lined valuables pocket, a padded air-mesh strap, and water-resistant fabric. They do not print a paddle count or a ball count, so this guide does not invent one. The stocking page already uses this sling as the recreational step-up from a gym sack.

## Court backpack

This is the league bag. You want two to four paddles, a pair of shoes that never touch the clean clothes, a bottle, and a fence hook if the bag waits on the fence between games. A ventilated or vented shoe bay matters on a two-day event. A shoe pocket that is only "separate" still keeps grit off the paddles. It does not promise airflow.

**HEAD Tour Pickleball Backpack 22 L ($$).** HEAD's US product page lists 22 liters, one racquet compartment for up to three racquets, a dedicated shoe compartment, a fence hook, two open accessory pockets, and two zippered accessory pockets, including one described for a phone. The name says Tour. The volume is a session pack, not a tournament duffel. HEAD does not call the shoe compartment ventilated.

**JOOLA Vision II Deluxe Pickleball Backpack ($$$).** Sporting-goods listings that reprint the JOOLA spec sheet, and JOOLA's own collection price, put this in the $$$ band. Those sheets print up to four paddles in a dedicated padded compartment, more than ten pockets, a ventilated shoe compartment, a fence hook, a laptop sleeve up to 17 inches, a side mesh bottle pocket, a polyester shell, and dimensions of 20 by 12 by 8 inches. This is the court backpack on this page that actually prints the word ventilated.

**ONIX Pro Team Pickleball Backpack ($$$).** ONIX lists durable polyester, 19 inches long by 14 inches wide by 9 inches high, four zippered pockets including a shoe and wet-storage compartment, a padded paddle compartment, padded shoulder straps, a top carry handle, side mesh bottle pockets, and a front organizer. ONIX does not print a paddle count and does not print a fence hook. Buy it when wet shoes are the problem and you can live without a hook.

**Selkirk Team Backpack ($$$$$).** The catalog lines print a compact backpack that holds up to six paddles, a spacious main compartment with a 15-inch laptop sleeve, and a +V11 Max Polyfiber shell. They do not print a shoe tunnel or a fence hook. Treat it as the premium paddle-and-laptop backpack, not as the ventilated shoe bag.

## Tour bag

Choose a tour bag when the day is a drive, a bracket, a change of clothes, and more than one paddle you care about keeping out of a hot trunk. You pay for volume and for pockets that separate paddles, shoes, and laundry. You also carry the empty weight.

**JOOLA Tour Elite Pickleball Bag ($$).** The catalog lines print designated pockets for four paddles, eight exterior zippered compartments, four interior pockets, a key tether, and a back-panel sleeve so the straps tuck away and the bag converts from backpack to duffel. Those lines do not print a thermal lining, a shoe compartment, or a fence hook. It is the smaller tour-style listing in this catalog, not the flagship duffle.

**CRBN Pro Team Tour Bag ($$$$$).** CRBN's product page lists room for six paddles in two padded, thermal-lined side pockets (three each), a vented shoe compartment, a top thermal-lined pocket that can hold snacks or drinks, a large center pouch, a lower compartment for dirty clothes, coated metal fence hooks that tuck away, a weatherproof shell, and a built-in zip-off sling. The shoulder straps can also carry it as a duffle. CRBN lists the empty bag at 3 pounds and the size at 23 inches long, 12 inches high, and 14 inches wide. The list price we checked sits above $150, which is why the band is $$$$$. Confirm the cart. A thermal lining is not a tested temperature rating.

## Court add-ons that ride in the bag

Protective eyewear and a raw-carbon eraser are not bag styles, and they are not this page's stocking list. They live in the [accessories catalog](/gear/accessories).

- **[ONIX Falcon eyewear](/gear/accessories#acc-onix-falcon-eyewear) ($).** ONIX lists polycarbonate lenses that block 99.9 percent of UV, with anti-scratch and anti-fog treatments, in clear, smoke, and blue tint. The frame is nylon, the set includes an adjustable strap and a microfiber pouch, and ONIX says the glasses meet or exceed ASTM F803-11.
- **[CRBN paddle eraser](/gear/accessories#acc-crbn-paddle-eraser) ($).** CRBN lists a rubber block, 2 by 2 by 1 inch and 1.5 ounces, for carbon-fiber faces and other raw peel-ply surfaces, including kevlar. It contains latex. CRBN does not recommend it on a face that already has an aftermarket coating, spray, or paint.

How edge tape and overgrips work is the [accessories buying guide](/guides/pickleball-accessories-buying-guide). If the gift has to fit a stocking, start there and on the stocking page, not with a tour bag.

## What to check on the live listing

- **Fence hook.** Printed on the Franklin sling, JOOLA Vision II Deluxe, HEAD 22 L, and CRBN. Named in the Mangrove description. Not printed on the ONIX Pro Team backpack.
- **Shoe bay.** "Ventilated" or "vented" is a stronger claim than "shoe compartment." Only Vision II Deluxe and CRBN use that wording here.
- **Thermal sleeve.** Only CRBN prints thermal-lined paddle pockets. Do not assume a black lining in a photo is insulation.
- **Paddle count.** If the listing does not print one, do not guess from the photo. ONIX and Mangrove are the two gaps on this page.
- **Empty weight.** CRBN prints 3 pounds empty. The other bags here do not print an empty weight in the lines we store, so this guide does not invent one.

Pair the bag with a paddle from the [paddles catalog](/gear/paddles) only after the capacity is settled. A flagship paddle in a sling that has no printed paddle sleeve is a scratch waiting to happen.
