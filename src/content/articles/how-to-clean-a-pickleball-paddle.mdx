## Why outdoor ball dust kills spin on peel-ply

Raw peel-ply is a textured carbon surface, not a smooth paint. The CRBN eraser listing is the catalog line that says **raw peel-ply**. It is made to lift ball residue, dirt, and light scuffs off carbon-fiber faces, and the same block is listed for other raw peel-ply surfaces, including kevlar.

These paddles are the raw-carbon examples. They do not all print the word peel-ply. They do print the face you maintain with that eraser:

- **Six Zero Double Black Diamond Control** prints a raw T700 carbon face celebrated for grit and spin longevity, plus control-tuned foam edge walls. Upper band ($$$$$). Millimeters are not printed.
- **Vatic Pro Prism Flash** prints a T700 raw carbon face, a 16mm poly core, foam-injected edge walls, and cold-molded control/touch. Band ($$$$).
- **Holbrook Mav Pro 2.0** is the listing that prints **T700 3K raw carbon** with enhanced grit for spin, plus a 16mm dual-reactive polypropylene honeycomb core. Upper band ($$$$$). The description positions thermoformed carbon. That mold sentence is separate from the face sentence.

Outdoor dust is the usual reason a textured face feels slick while the carbon is still there. The powder packs into the low spots of the texture. The next ball meets dust, so brush and roll lose bite. You can see it as a pale film in the sweet spot after a few games on concrete or asphalt. Wiping that film off is maintenance. It is not a grit score, and this catalog prints no RPM.

### Rubber eraser versus a damp microfiber

Use the eraser when the face is raw peel-ply and the texture looks packed, not polished away.

The CRBN block contains **latex**. Printed size is **2 inches by 2 inches by 1 inch**. Net weight is **1.5 ounces**. Price band is **$**. Rub with short strokes and light pressure. You are lifting residue, not sanding carbon. A dry microfiber picks up what the block loosens so you do not grind crumbs back into the texture.

A damp microfiber is the whole method on smooth, painted, sprayed, and graphite faces. On a raw peel-ply face it is only the follow-up, and only when a dry cloth left a film. Wring it until it does not drip. Water that runs into the edge or under the handle is how a honeycomb core and a grip go soft.

### Clean a raw peel-ply face

1. **Read the face line on the paddle you own.** Raw T700, T700 raw carbon, or T700 3K raw carbon stays in this section. A coating, spray, paint, fiberglass, or graphite line moves to the water-only section. The eraser listing says CRBN does not recommend the block on an aftermarket coating, spray, or paint.
2. **Knock the loose dust off outside.** Face down, tap the handle, and let the powder fall away from the court and your bag.
3. **Rub the eraser in short strokes across the sweet spot,** then the rest of the face. Stop when the pale film is gone and the texture feels like texture. More pressure does not create grit.
4. **Wipe with a dry microfiber.** If a haze remains, one pass with a barely damp cloth, then dry immediately.
5. **Check the edge and the handle before you zip the cover.** Dust on the face is not the same job as a slick grip or a scraped edge. Those are the next section.

### Water-only wipe

1. **Start with a clean microfiber and plain water.** No soap, no spray cleaner, no alcohol.
2. **Wring the cloth until it is cool and barely damp.** If it drips, it is too wet for a honeycomb core or a wrapped handle.
3. **Wipe the face in one direction, then dry it with a second cloth.** Include the edge guard only if you can keep water out of any seam.
4. **Stop.** A second product will not add spin to a smooth fiberglass face. Radical Elite already prints a smoother face and less spin. That is the build, not a dirty build.

<Callout type="warning" title="Keep these off every paddle face">
Solvents, alcohol, acetone, melamine foam, and dish soap stay off the face. Soap film is slick. Melamine foam abrades. A hose or a bucket soak can swell a honeycomb core and the handle. Onix Z5, Radical Elite, Mav Pro 2.0, and the Diadem ICON all print a honeycomb core. Perseus 16mm and Helix Pro print thermoformed builds. None of them want a soak.
</Callout>

### Heat, honeycomb, and a closed car

Cleaning day is also a storage check. A thermoformed paddle and a honeycomb-core paddle do not belong in a closed trunk or on a dashboard in the sun. Heat can soften glue. A core that crushes or a face that lifts will not come back with an eraser or a damp cloth.

- **Selkirk SLK Helix Pro** prints a thermoformed core.
- **Mav Pro 2.0** prints a polypropylene honeycomb core, and the description positions thermoformed carbon.
- **Onix Z5** prints a Nomex honeycomb core. **Radical Elite** prints an OTC honeycomb polymer core. **Diadem ICON** prints an 8mm PP honeycomb core.

### Worn peel-ply

If the sweet spot is polished smooth, the eraser has nothing to uncover. More rubbing only shortens whatever texture is left. That paddle’s spin life is over even if the core still feels lively. Replace it with a face line you can read: raw T700, T700 raw carbon, or T700 3K raw carbon. ### Core crush and delamination

Pop that stays flat after the face is clean is a core problem. A dead spot, a soft patch, or an edge that is separating will not improve with water or rubber.
