## Indoor vs outdoor: what's actually different

- **Outdoor balls** are harder, slightly heavier, and have more (and smaller) holes — usually around 40 — so they resist wind and hold up on rough asphalt or concrete.
- **Indoor balls** are softer, lighter, and have fewer, larger holes — typically around 26 — built for the smoother bounce of a gym floor.

Play the wrong type in the wrong setting and you'll get erratic bounces (outdoor ball indoors) or a ball that cracks fast (indoor ball outdoors, especially in cold weather — a real consideration for Canadian courts in shoulder-season months).

## Buying tips

- **Buy in bulk for practice.** Balls crack and scuff faster than most gear; a 12-pack brings the per-ball cost down versus singles or tiny packs.
- **Cold weather makes outdoor balls brittle.** If you're playing outdoor courts into fall, expect more cracking than in peak summer — it's the material, not necessarily a defect.
- **Tournament-approved matters if you compete.** Check that whatever you buy for league or tournament play carries USA Pickleball approval.
