## Why a kitchen exchange is an eye problem

Most points at the non-volley zone end with a block, a reset, or a sudden speed-up from a few feet away. You also get balls that were never aimed at you: a mishit off your partner's paddle, a volley that clips the edge, a ball that kicks off the tape. Outdoor and indoor balls both do this. The hole pattern changes the flight. It does not make a fashion lens into a protector.

## Three labels that are not the same thing

**ASTM F803** is the specification for eye protectors in selected sports, including racket and paddle sports. The test is about a ball or a stick hitting the protector, and about the lens staying in the frame instead of releasing toward the eye. ONIX states that Falcon eyewear meets or exceeds **ASTM F803-11**, the 2011 revision of that standard. That sentence is a manufacturer claim in our catalog. The glasses you receive should carry the mark. If the live listing does not show it, do not fill in the gap yourself.

**ANSI Z87.1** is the industrial and educational standard. It covers workplace impact, splash, and dust. A Z87 stamp can mean a serious shop lens. It does not, by itself, mean the frame was tested the way a paddle-sport protector is tested under F803. Some pairs are marked for both. Buy the mark your sport uses, and read the one printed on the listing.

**Casual sunglasses** can block glare and, on some pairs, UV. They are not built as sports protectors. Glass and brittle plastic can shatter. A lens that is not locked into a sports frame can pop inward on impact, which is the failure the retention test in a sports standard is there to catch. UV-blocking fashion lenses are still fashion lenses.

## Fog, sweat, and where the pouch goes

Anti-fog on this listing is a treatment on the polycarbonate lens, next to the anti-scratch treatment. Vents are a different feature: slots that let moist air leave the lens. Lots of court glasses advertise both. This SKU prints the treatment and does not print vents. If you fog up in a still gym, a coating helps until it wears off. It is not a promise of airflow the listing never names.

The slip point in summer is the nose. Looking down for a dink drops a slick frame into your line of sight. The Falcon lines print soft non-slip nose pieces, TRP rubber side-arm padding, soft ear pads, and an adjustable elastic strap. That is the retention hardware in the catalog. Sweat can still overwhelm it on a humid afternoon. Confirm the fit against the printed width, and do not expect a second size.

The microfiber storage pouch is included. Use it. A loose lens in the same pocket as outdoor balls picks up grit, and grit is what an anti-scratch coating is trying to resist. Two bags in this catalog can carry that pouch without this page turning into a bag ranking:

- The [Franklin Sports sling](/gear/bags#bag-franklin-sling) is in the **$** band. It prints a felt-lined soft pocket for personal devices, plus a fence hook. The pocket is described for a device, not for glasses.
- The [JOOLA Vision II Deluxe backpack](/gear/bags#bag-joola-vision-ii-deluxe) is in the **$$$** band. It prints more than ten compartments, including a ventilated shoe section and a side mesh water-bottle pocket. It does not print an eyewear pocket. Keep the pouch out of the shoe bay.
