## Lightweight vs midweight vs heavy at a glance

There is no “better” class — only a better match for how you already miss, and for the **ounces the live listing actually prints**. The bands below are retailer conventions, not a lab standard. Charts cut lightweight at 7.3 oz or ~7.6 oz; midweight usually runs from that cut to about 8.3 oz; heavy is the band above that. This catalog’s printed static weights sit around **7.6–8.1 oz**.

## What the ounce line actually means

**Static weight** is the scale number — the oz (or oz range) on the spec sheet. **Swing weight** is how heavy that paddle feels when you accelerate it. Mass out in the head feels heavier than the same ounces near the handle. This catalog prints static ounces on some SKUs; it does **not** print swing-weight numbers. Do not invent one.

Catalog copy is not standardized:

- **Printed ounces** — HEAD Radical Pro (2024) `~8.1 oz`; Radical Tour GR `~7.9 oz`; Radical Tour XL `~7.6 oz`; Babolat XPLR `13mm, ~7.8 oz`; Babolat RBEL `14mm, ~7.6 oz`; niupipo `Lightweight ~7.6 oz`; Venom `~7.75-8.0 oz`.
- **A range on one line** — Selkirk SLK Latitude (original listing) prints `Lightweight 7.6-8.0oz`. That range sits in the conventional **midweight** band even though the headline says lightweight.
- **Lite or Mid on one family page** — Diadem ICON Performance lists **Lite (7.7oz) or Mid (8.0oz)**. Confirm which option is in the cart.
- **The word without a number** — ROYCELONG T700 carbon says **Mid-weight, quiet** and does not print ounces. GAMMA Quantum Series 5.0 lists **multiple style/weight variants** on one page. Omit those from a class decision until the cart shows oz.
- **Overgrip and tungsten** — an [overgrip](/guides/pickleball-accessories-buying-guide) adds a little static mass and changes handle feel. [Tungsten tape](/guides/pickleball-accessories-buying-guide) is how you add plow-through to a paddle you already like. Neither one turns a guess into a spec.

**Thickness is not weight.** Millimeters describe core depth; ounces describe mass. RBEL is a **14mm** listing at **~7.6 oz**. The Radical Pro (2024) is a **15mm** listing at **~8.1 oz**. Diadem ICON is an **8mm** core with Lite/Mid ounce options. A 16mm paddle in this catalog is not automatically heavier than a 13mm — most 16mm SKUs here simply do not print ounces. Solve weight here, then [thickness](/guides/14mm-vs-16mm-pickleball-paddles).

## Buy midweight when you want the default all-court paddle

Choose **midweight** if **all-court rec is the job**: mixed-skill doubles, a first upgrade from wood or a toy set, and any night where you still need both kitchen touch and a put-away. You are not chasing the lightest swing or the most plow-through. That is the default in [how to choose a paddle](/guides/how-to-choose-a-pickleball-paddle).

Skip midweight as a *requirement* if the current paddle already feels like too much mass in the hand, or if you already center the ball and can name the plow-through you are missing.

Catalog anchors with an **explicit mid-band ounce line** (confirm the live SKU):

- **HEAD Radical Pro (2024)** — listing prints **`~8.1 oz`** with a 15mm control-oriented core and a Dynamic Power Shape. The clearest all-court midweight number in this catalog ($$). Confirm today’s ounces; 8.1 still sits below a conventional heavy cut.
- **HEAD Radical Tour GR** — **`~7.9 oz`**, graphite face, thicker OTC honeycomb, Control Stabilizer Technology. Advanced listing aimed at power with added stability ($$$$). Mid band, not a heavy SKU.
- **Babolat XPLR** — **`13mm, ~7.8 oz`**, fiberglass composite, polypropylene honeycomb. The feature line also says **Lightweight/easy swing**. Treat the **printed 7.8 oz** as conventional midweight, not a sub-7.6 light frame ($$$$). Thickness plus weight — do not flatten it into “just a 14mm.”
- **Pickle-Ball, Inc. Venom** — **`~7.75-8.0 oz`**, composite face, **head-weighted for power**. Midweight on the scale with mass biased toward the head. Legacy paddle; **stock varies**. Not a heavy-band SKU.
- **Selkirk SLK Latitude (original listing)** — **`Lightweight 7.6-8.0oz`** on a 13mm SX4 polymer core. The printed range is the conventional mid band. This is the original Latitude SKU — not the pricier Latitude 13mm/bundle listing. Entry Selkirk ($$).

## Buy lightweight when you want hand speed or less mass in the arm

Choose **lightweight** if **faster hands are the job** and you can already name why the current paddle feels like too much mass. You give up some plow-through. You gain a quicker swing at the kitchen. This is also the honest pick when arm fatigue after a couple of games is the complaint — comfort, not a medical claim.

This is **not** the default for new players. A light frame will not teach drops. [Seniors](/guides/best-pickleball-gear-for-seniors) and mixed rec still usually start midweight unless the player already knows they want less mass.

Catalog anchors with an **explicit light-end ounce line** (confirm the live SKU):

- **niupipo fiberglass** — listing prints **`Lightweight ~7.6 oz`**, polypropylene honeycomb, low-profile edge guard. Proven budget traditional build for the first upgrade from wood ($$). See [paddles under $100](/guides/pickleball-paddles-under-100). Light-end ounces — not a 16mm carbon twin.
- **Babolat RBEL** — **`14mm, ~7.6 oz`**, graphite skin, RPM Max Grit, EVA-enclosed polypropylene honeycomb. Feature line says **Light/maneuverable**. The catalog’s explicit 14mm *and* a light-end ounce line ($$$). Thickness is a separate fork.
- **HEAD Radical Tour XL** — **`~7.6 oz`**, carbon graphite face, elongated power shape. Advanced listing ($$$$). Light-end ounces **and** extra length — do not flatten it into “just a lightweight paddle.” Solve [shape](/guides/elongated-vs-widebody-pickleball-paddles) after weight.

## Buy heavy only if you already center — this catalog is thin

Choose **heavy** if **plow-through is the job** and you already hit the center: drives, blocks against pace, and a swing that does not need a lighter head to stay on time. You trade hand speed for mass behind the ball.

**This catalog does not currently list a SKU in the conventional heavy static band (~8.4 oz and up).** The heaviest *printed* number here is the HEAD Radical Pro (2024) at **~8.1 oz**, which is still midweight on common charts. Venom is **head-weighted** at **~7.75–8.0 oz** — more mass in the head, not a heavy-band static weight.

If you want more plow-through than those listings, do not invent an 8.5 oz paddle. **Add [tungsten tape](/guides/pickleball-accessories-buying-guide) to a midweight you already center**, then confirm the unweighted ounces on the live SKU. That is the honest heavy path in this catalog.

## Nearby labels that are not this decision

Shoppers mash four different stories into one cart. Keep them separate:

- **Thickness** — 14mm vs 16mm changes dwell vs pop, not the ounce class. RBEL is both 14mm **and** ~7.6 oz. XPLR is 13mm **and** ~7.8 oz. Solve weight here, then [thickness](/guides/14mm-vs-16mm-pickleball-paddles).
- **Shape** — elongated vs widebody changes how you miss. Radical Tour XL is light-end ounces **and** elongated. Solve weight here, then [shape](/guides/elongated-vs-widebody-pickleball-paddles).
- **Construction** — thermoformed vs cold-molded is the mold, not the scale. See [thermoformed vs traditional](/guides/thermoformed-vs-traditional-pickleball-paddles).
- **Power vs control shortlists** — those guides sort by job. A midweight can be the control pick or the power pick. Weight is the filter, not the whole answer.

## Buying checklist

1. Name the job: **all-court default** (midweight), **hand speed / less mass** (lightweight), or **plow-through** (heavy, only if you already center). Do not name a pro.
2. If you cannot describe your mishits yet, default **midweight**.
3. Read the listing for the actual ounces. “Lightweight,” “power,” and “mid” are not enough. If the family offers Lite and Mid, pick one SKU.
4. Shortlist **one** paddle from that camp in the tables above. Match [thickness](/guides/14mm-vs-16mm-pickleball-paddles), [shape](/guides/elongated-vs-widebody-pickleball-paddles), and [construction](/guides/thermoformed-vs-traditional-pickleball-paddles) after weight.
5. Confirm exact SKU, listed ounces, seller, approval language, and price on the store — then stop shopping. If you still want more plow-through than this catalog prints, add tungsten to that midweight instead of inventing a heavy SKU.

<Callout type="warning" title="Weight is not a skill upgrade">
A lightweight paddle will not fix late preparation. A heavy paddle will not give you kitchen hands. Buy the mass you already play, not the one a highlight reel uses.
</Callout>
