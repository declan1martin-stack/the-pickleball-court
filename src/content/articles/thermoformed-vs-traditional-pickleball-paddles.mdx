Construction is the paddle decision most shoppers skip — then blame the brand. **Thermoformed** frames are heat-formed (often a fused edge or unibody shell). **Traditional** frames are faces bonded to a honeycomb core the older way, including **cold-pressed / cold-molded** control builds and classic graphite or fiberglass. ## What “thermoformed” actually means on a listing

**Thermoformed** is manufacturing language, not a skill rating. Heat and a mold fuse some combination of face, core, and edge so the paddle plays more like one piece. Catalog copy is not standardized:

- **Thermoformed edge** — the JOOLA Perseus 16mm listing. Pop with usable resets; $$$$$ band.
- **Thermoformed construction** — the Six Zero Ruby 16mm listing (16mm, hybrid shape).
- **Unibody thermoformed** — the Pickln Piknix Pro listing (T700 Toray raw carbon). Value band ($$$).
- **Thermoformed core** — the Selkirk SLK Helix Pro listing. Beginner-to-improver step-up after wood or basic fiberglass ($$).

Do not flatten those four phrases into one paddle. A thermoformed-core Helix Pro is not a unibody Piknix, and neither is a Perseus-class edge.

<Callout type="warning" title="Carbon is not a construction">
Raw carbon, CAS, and “T700” describe the face. Thermoformed describes the mold. The Scorpeus 3S listing is wide-body raw carbon — it does not say thermoformed. CRBN Power lists foam-injected edges, not a thermoformed shell. Read the build line, then shop.
</Callout>

## Buy thermoformed when you want a connected, modern frame

Choose thermoformed if **you already hit the center** and want a stiffer, more connected feel with extra pop on drives and speed-ups. You are trading a more familiar, often more forgiving traditional sweet spot for a fused-edge or unibody response.

Skip it if you still mistime resets, pop dinks up, or play mixed-skill rec where contact is late. A heat-formed shell will not teach the kitchen.

Catalog anchors with **explicit thermoformed language** (confirm the live SKU):

- **Pickln Piknix Pro** — T700 Toray raw carbon **unibody thermoformed** build; foam-injected edge walls aimed at a CRBN-style sweet spot. The honest value upgrade when you want raw-carbon spin without flagship pricing ($$$).
- **JOOLA Perseus 16mm** — **16mm thermoformed edge** for pop without giving up touch resets. Power-oriented Perseus-family frame for players who already drive with intent. Upper catalog band ($$$$$).
- **Six Zero Ruby 16mm** — **16mm thermoformed construction** listed as a hybrid shape (reach plus forgiveness). Intermediate carbon step-up — not a beginner block paddle.
- **Selkirk SLK Helix Pro** — **thermoformed core** with a large sweet spot; carbon + fiberglass hybrid face. Lower catalog band ($$). Treat it as a first modern-construction paddle, not a tour unibody.
- **Holbrook Mav Pro 2.0** — features list a T700 3K raw carbon face and 16mm dual-reactive polypropylene honeycomb. Catalog **description** positions it as a thermoformed-carbon step-up under flagship JOOLA pricing. Confirm the live listing before you treat it as a Perseus-class unibody.

## Buy traditional (cold-pressed) when you want familiar touch

Choose traditional if **forgiveness and a known sweet-spot feel are the job**: first upgrades from wood, mixed-skill doubles, and kitchen-first rec. Faces are bonded to a polymer or Nomex honeycomb core. **Cold-molded / cold-pressed** is the control-oriented subset of that family — not a marketing synonym for “cheap.”

This is the default for new players and anyone whose current paddle already feels fine except for weight or grip. It is also the honest pick if you live at the kitchen and win with hands, not with baseline drives.

- **Vatic Pro Prism Flash 16mm** — T700 raw carbon, 16mm poly core, foam-injected edge walls, and explicit **cold-molded for control/touch**. The clean traditional-carbon counterweight to unibody thermoformed frames.
- **Onix Graphite Z5** — classic widebody graphite on a Nomex honeycomb core. Control-oriented, large sweet spot. A shape-and-feel lesson, not a budget toy ($$$$).

## Nearby labels that are not this decision

Shoppers mash three different stories into one cart. Keep them separate:

- **Foam-injected edges** — CRBN Power and the Piknix Pro both list foam-edge walls. That is perimeter feel, not proof of a thermoformed unibody. CRBN Power’s listing does **not** say thermoformed.
- **Foam-core / full-foam** — a core family, explained in [foam-core paddles](/guides/pickleball-paddle-foam-cores-explained). Not a synonym for thermoformed.
- **Raw carbon widebody** — JOOLA Scorpeus 3S is a kitchen-first 3S carbon shape. Strong paddle, different question. Use it after you pick construction, or when you want JOOLA carbon without a thermoformed claim on the sheet.

## Buying checklist

1. Name the job: **connected pop** (thermoformed) or **familiar touch / forgiveness** (traditional). Do not name a pro.
2. If you cannot describe your mishits yet, default **traditional** (Halo Control XL or a solid fiberglass).
3. Read the listing for the actual word: thermoformed, cold-molded, graphite, or fiberglass. “Carbon” is not enough.
4. Shortlist **one** paddle from that camp in the tables above. Match thickness and [shape](/guides/elongated-vs-widebody-pickleball-paddles) after the build.
5. Confirm exact SKU, seller, approval language, and price on the store — then stop shopping.

<Callout type="warning" title="The mold is not a skill upgrade">
A thermoformed paddle will not fix late preparation. A cold-molded graphite will not give you unibody pop. Buy the construction you already play, not the one a highlight reel uses.
</Callout>
