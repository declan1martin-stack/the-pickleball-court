## Court comfort and break-in

Choose this lane when the miss is how the shoe feels for the session you actually play: a weekend hour, or several sessions a week. The split inside K-Swiss is printed on the cards. Court Express names the entry-level, part-time, weekend player. Hypercourt Express 2 names Durawrap Flex and says there is no break-in period. Express Light is the frequent-player shoe the Court Express cards point to, and it exists here only as a women's listing.

### Hypercourt Express 2

Buy **Hypercourt Express 2** when you play often enough to care about the performance court shoe, and you want the listing that prints a no-break-in upper. Both the men's and women's cards list the same five lines:

- **AOSTA 7.0** high-density outsole, durability from heel to toe.
- **180 PSC**, a 180-degree plantar support chassis for mid-foot stability. That is a midfoot feature name. It is not the plantar-fasciitis-relief tag, and neither Hypercourt carries that tag.
- **Surgelite** cushioning, described as lightweight, flexible, and durable.
- **Durawrap Flex**, protection and flex so there is no break-in period.
- **OrthoLite** sock liner for moisture management.

Both badges are $$$$. Both descriptions say skill level intermediate. Comfort tag on both: extra-cushioning. The product name says tennis shoe. The men's description calls it a lighter performance court shoe for tennis-to-pickleball play. The women's description calls it a stable, cushioned crossover with AOSTA rubber. Neither listing prints a gram weight, so "lighter" stays the men's description's word. It is not a measured ounce.

### Court Express

Buy **Court Express** when the week is recreational and you want the entry court shoe, not the performance upper. Men's and women's cards share the player line: designed for the entry-level, part-time, weekend player, and frequent players are pointed at Express Light. They also share **K-EVA** midsole cushioning, **AOSTA II** high-density non-marking rubber from heel to toe, and an **OrthoLite** sock liner.

The bands are not the same. Men's Court Express is $$$. Women's Court Express is $$, the lowest band on this page. Both say skill level beginner. Comfort tag on both: extra-cushioning. Neither prints a wide toe box, a no-break-in sentence, or a width letter. Non-marking AOSTA II is the indoor-friendly outsole line. Confirm the sole in the cart if your week is outdoor acrylic.

### Express Light

Buy **Express Light** when you want the women's frequent-play K-Swiss. The description calls it a lighter women's pickleball shoe with a secure heel, DragGuard rubber, and a court-ready wide toe box. Skill level: intermediate. Band $$$. Comfort tags: wide-fit and extra-cushioning.

The feature lines to shop against:

- A wide toe box, with heels that stay secure on quick moves.
- **DragGuard 7.0** rubber at high-wear toes and heels. The outsole line also says it keeps grip.
- **Durawrap** upper, a tough overlay for toe-drag wear. This is not the Hypercourt Durawrap Flex no-break-in sentence.
- A sizing note that going up one-half size is often ideal for toe room, whether you want a standard or wide women's fit. That is the listing's note. Confirm it on the size chart.
- **OrthoLite** moisture management, in the all-court outsole bullet, next to special EVA cushioning.

There is no men's Express Light in this catalog. If you are a frequent men's player, the performance K-Swiss on this page is Hypercourt Express 2.
