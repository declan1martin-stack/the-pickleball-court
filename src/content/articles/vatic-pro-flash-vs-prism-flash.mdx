## How they play

**Flash** is for aggressive players who want power and spin. The face is heat-textured raw carbon. That texture grips the ball when you brush it, so a drive, a roll, or a speed-up leaves with more spin. The hybrid power shape is the frame you swing through once you already find the center and you want pace on that swing. The edge is carbon-encased.

You notice it when the rally leaves the soft game. A roll from the transition zone, a drive at the return, and a speed-up after you are set at the kitchen are the swings this face is for. The extra spin is what helps those balls come back down, instead of floating long. If that is how you try to win the point, the Flash is the match.

**Prism Flash** is for control-oriented players. It is cold-molded for control and touch. On the [thermoformed vs traditional](/guides/thermoformed-vs-traditional-pickleball-paddles) guide, that build means a more familiar feel and easier touch at the kitchen. A dink, a drop, or a reset is simpler to place because the paddle is built to help you take pace off the ball. The face is still raw carbon, so this is a carbon paddle aimed at placement.

You notice it in the soft part of the point. The third-shot drop has to land short, a dink has to stay low, and a reset has to answer a speed-up and come back down at the kitchen. Those are the shots this paddle is for.

## Buy one

Most players need one of these, not a pair. The two paddles split a point into an attacking job and a kitchen job, so the frame you buy should be the job you do more often, or the job you currently lose. Attacks that float, with little spin, are the Flash. Dinks and resets that sit up are the Prism Flash.

The Prism Flash is a full control paddle. The lower price is a different job, not a discount on the Flash. Spend the extra on the Flash only when your soft game is already in and you want more bite on the ball you attack.
