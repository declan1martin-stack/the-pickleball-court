This is gear guidance, not medical advice. Sharp or persistent pain deserves a clinician, not just a new SKU.

## What to look for

Heel cushioning, secure midfoot, court outsole, and a ride that does not slam your joints on outdoor Canadian hard courts.

## How to use this guide on court

Read once for the framework, then shortlist two products max. Take those names to a demo day, a club buddy’s bag, or an return-friendly store order. Play a soft session and a competitive session before you decide. If a paddle or shoe only feels good when you are fresh, it is not your match.

Canadian outdoor conditions deserve a special note: cold mornings deaden balls, dusty acrylic eats shoe rubber, and wind punishes lofty drops. Buy gear that stays predictable when the environment is not. That usually means a forgiving sweet spot, stable footwear, and — if you build a home court — a portable net that holds center height after repeated setups.

## Buying checklist

1. Define the job (control, power, spin, comfort, portability).
2. Set a price tier, not a fantasy budget.
3. Confirm seller reputation and return window.
4. Ignore hype specs you cannot verify.
5. Re-evaluate after five real sessions, not one highlight rally.

## Common mistakes we see

People upgrade paddles when they need footwork. People buy running shoes because they “already own them.” People pick the heaviest power frame in the store because a YouTube thumbnail looked aggressive. The fix is boring and effective: match gear to the week you actually play.

## Durability and maintenance basics

Wipe grit off faces and outsoles. Do not leave graphite/carbon paddles baking in a hot car if you can avoid it. Retire shoes when the lateral rubber is rounded slick — ankle stability disappears before the upper looks dead. For nets, dry moisture before storage and inspect center straps for stretch.

## Extra practical notes for Canadian players

Club booking culture, shared carts, and mixed-skill round robins mean your gear has to work for imperfect warmups. Arrive with shoes that do not need a three-week break-in and a paddle you can dink with before your shoulder is hot. If you travel between provinces for tournaments, keep a spare overgrip and verify your portable net’s packed dimensions fit your trunk without crushing the frame.
