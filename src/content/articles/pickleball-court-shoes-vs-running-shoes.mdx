## Why the outsole job is different

A road or treadmill shoe is built to **roll forward**. The foam and the outsole pattern favor a stride, not a plant-and-cut.

Pickleball on hard court is a series of **lateral cuts, split steps, and kitchen stops**, then a quiet reset. The shoe has to hold the outside edge when you lunge for a wide ball. Soft road rubber flexes the wrong way on that stop.

This catalog does **not** stock running shoes. Every shoe below is a court shoe or a tennis-named court shoe with a court outsole line. We do not invent a stack height, heel-to-toe drop, or gram weight the listing does not print.

## Tennis court shoes versus running shoes

Tennis court shoes often transfer. Running shoes do not. That split also shows up on [pickleball vs tennis](/guides/pickleball-vs-tennis).

In this catalog, **Hypercourt Express 2** (men and women) and **Gel-Dedicate 5** are named tennis shoes with court descriptions. **Court Express** and **Express Light** are named pickleball shoes. None of those are road runners. Confirm the outsole photo for your gym floor or outdoor acrylic before you treat a tennis name as automatic indoor approval.

## When a running shoe is still fine

- One driveway session or a free clinic while you decide if you will keep playing.
- Walking to the court — then change into court shoes for the games.

Once you booked a season, a weekly open play, or a club night, the running shoe is no longer the plan. Court shoes protect the stop more than a paddle upgrade raises your rating. The [fall league starter kit](/guides/fall-league-pickleball-starter-kit) says the same thing in the shoe slot.
