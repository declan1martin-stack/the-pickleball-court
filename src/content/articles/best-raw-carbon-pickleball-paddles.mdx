## Who this is for

You belong here if the reason for a new paddle is the **face the listing prints**:

- You already brush low-to-high, and you want **raw carbon**, **T700**, or a grit/spin-longevity line you can quote.
- You are leaving a fiberglass face or a smooth graphite face, and the miss is bite the catalog describes as grit, texture, or spin longevity.
- You will still separate shape and build: elongated power, a wide-body kitchen face, cold-molded touch, an edgeless T700, or a budget first carbon.
- You will confirm the seller and the live price. The badge is a band.

You do **not** belong here for a first paddle with no face preference. ## What “raw carbon” means on this page

“Raw carbon” gets pasted onto three different catalog ideas. Separate them before you spend flagship money.

### Grit language, with no lab score

Grit and texture count only when the listing prints them. Double Black Diamond Control prints **grit and spin longevity**. Mav Pro 2.0 prints **enhanced grit for spin**. Vatic Flash prints **heat-textured** raw carbon for spin. Scorpeus 3S prints **bite**. This catalog has **no grit score**, **no RPM**, and **no wear-hour number**. Do not upgrade a marketing phrase into a lab result. How long a texture lasts is not printed here.

### Face line versus the mold

Raw carbon is the face sentence. Thermoformed, cold-molded, and foam are build sentences. The [thermoformed guide](/guides/thermoformed-vs-traditional-pickleball-paddles) already separates them: carbon is a face fiber, thermoformed is how the paddle is formed.

- **Piknix Pro** prints both: T700 Toray raw carbon **and** unibody thermoformed.
- **Prism Flash** prints T700 raw carbon **and** cold-molded for control/touch.
- **Mav Pro 2.0** features print T700 3K raw carbon and a 16mm honeycomb. The **description** positions thermoformed carbon. Confirm the live listing before you treat that description as a unibody claim.
- **Scorpeus 3S** prints raw carbon and does not say thermoformed.
- **Perseus 3S** prints charged/raw carbon and a 3S Propulsion Core. It does not say thermoformed.
- **JOOLA Perseus 16mm** prints a thermoformed edge and does not print raw carbon or T700. It stays off this buy list.

Foam at the edge is a third sentence. Read [foam cores explained](/guides/pickleball-paddle-foam-cores-explained) before you treat foam-injected walls as a grit score.

## Buying checklist

1. Name **one** job: grit and spin longevity, control or a wider face, edgeless T700, elongated raw-carbon power, or a budget first raw-carbon.
2. Require the **exact face words** on that SKU: raw carbon, raw T700, T700, or the ROYCELONG description that says raw-carbon. A carbon word alone is the [materials explainer](/guides/carbon-fiber-vs-fiberglass-paddles).
3. Read the **build sentence** second. Unibody thermoformed, cold-molded, foam edge, and foam core are different lines. Open the [thermoformed guide](/guides/thermoformed-vs-traditional-pickleball-paddles) before you infer a mold from the face.
4. Read **thickness only when it is printed**. Mav Pro, Prism Flash, Vatic Flash, Piknix, ROYCELONG, and Pro IV Perseus print 16mm. Volair prints 14mm and 16mm options. DBD Control, Perseus 3S, Scorpeus 3S, and Aero do not print millimeters. ROYCELONG’s “mid-weight” is not an ounce. Aero’s “lighter” is not an ounce.
5. If the description says “value” or “without flagship pricing,” check the **badge**. Piknix is $$$. ROYCELONG is $$. Prism Flash is $$$$. Mav Pro, Vatic Flash, and the JOOLA raw-carbon frames on this page are $$$$$.
6. Leave fiberglass-only faces and non-raw graphite off the cart. Leave Perseus 16mm on the [power guide](/guides/best-pickleball-paddles-for-power) unless that thermoformed listing is the one you meant.
7. If you cannot yet shape the ball, stop and use [beginner paddles](/guides/best-pickleball-paddles-for-beginners). A raw face will not supply the brush.
8. Confirm seller, approval language, shape (especially other Pro IV shapes), and **current store price** — then stop. $–$$$$$ badges are relative catalog bands, not live prices.
