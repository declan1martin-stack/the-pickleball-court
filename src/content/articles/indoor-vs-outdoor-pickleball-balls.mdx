Shoppers comparing **outdoor pickleball balls** with **indoor pickleball balls** are choosing hole pattern and plastic, then a pack that matches the court. A **40-hole outdoor ball** uses smaller holes and firmer plastic so it holds a line in wind and bounces on asphalt or concrete. A **26-hole indoor ball** uses larger holes and a lighter, softer feel so it stays controllable on gym wood or a sport-court floor. The wrong class changes the rally before the paddle does.

## 40-hole outdoor vs 26-hole indoor

Outdoor balls are the harder, wind-resistant class. More, smaller holes — the pattern players mean by **40-hole** — keep the ball from sailing on an open court. Firmer plastic is what gives that ball a usable bounce on rough asphalt and concrete. The trade-off is cold weather. Harder plastic gets brittle when the air drops, so outdoor balls crack more often than they do in warm weather. This catalog does not print a temperature, a crack count, or a ranked lifespan. Dura’s ball-life line says weather, play style, and court surface affect how long a sanctioned outdoor ball lasts, and that those balls do not last forever. Carry spares and retire a cracked ball.

Indoor balls are the softer control class. Fewer, larger holes — the pattern players mean by **26-hole** — lighten the flight and make the ball easier to place on a smooth gym floor. The only SKU here that prints the indoor number is the Franklin X-26: 26 beveled holes, EVA construction, 26 grams, and 74mm. Those two measurements belong to that listing. They are not printed on X-40, Dura Fast 40, or Fuse G2, so this page does not copy them across.

## Franklin X-40 for rec-club outdoor bulk

Choose the **Franklin X-40** when the session is outdoor open play, a club night, or practice that did not name another ball. You are buying the catalog’s outdoor **12-pack**, so one cracked ball does not end the round.

The listing prints **40 precise, machine-drilled holes** for spin and a balanced flight path, and **seamless one-piece construction** for concrete and asphalt. It also uses USAP-approved language and official-ball language for the US Open. That official-ball line is cut off in this catalog after “and USA,” so confirm the rest on the live listing instead of guessing the sentence. A separate line says the ball comes in bright, high-contrast colors; the color list itself is cut off. Description: the US club and tournament outdoor standard. Skill level: all. Band ($).

Skip it for a gym. That job is the X-26. Skip it when the event card already names Dura or ONIX.

## Buying checklist

1. **Check the surface.** Outdoor asphalt or concrete stays in the 40-hole class. Gym wood or indoor sport court is the 26-hole class. Look at the floor, not the paddle. Paddle context: [indoor vs outdoor paddles](/guides/indoor-vs-outdoor-pickleball-paddles).
2. **If the club, league, or event named a ball, buy that SKU.** A retail “official ball” line is not this weekend’s event list. For a sanctioned match, confirm the model on the [USA Pickleball approved equipment list](https://equipment.usapickleball.org/).
3. **If nobody named an outdoor ball,** default the Franklin X-40 12-pack for rec-club bulk.
4. **Pick the outdoor job if you are not in a gym.** Dura Fast 40 for fast tournament-style outdoor play (6-pack on the listing). ONIX Fuse G2 for a ready-out-of-the-box outdoor 3-pack. Brand details after that choice: [Franklin X-40 vs Dura Fast 40 vs ONIX](/guides/franklin-x40-vs-dura-fast-40-vs-onix).
5. **Use X-26 only indoors.** 26 beveled holes, EVA, 12-pack. Do not treat Fuse G2’s indoor/outdoor consistency line as a substitute.
6. **Match the pack to how many balls you will crack.** 12 for weekly outdoor or indoor club use. 6 when you specifically want Dura. 3 when you are testing Fuse G2. Confirm the count in the cart.
7. **Expect cold-weather cracking on outdoor balls without a printed temperature.** Retire a cracked ball. Do not invent a lifespan from the brand name.
8. **Read the band ($ or $$), then confirm seller, exact SKU, pack count, and the current price on the store.**
