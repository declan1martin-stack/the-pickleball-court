It's a busy stretch for competitive pickleball north of the border, and the next few weeks are shaping up to be some of the biggest of the Canadian season. Here's what moved between August 1 and 8, plus the marquee events worth circling on your calendar.

## CNPL Central Split hits London

The most immediate news is the Canadian National Pickleball League heading to London, Ontario. On August 6 the CNPL published its fan guide for the Central Split, which runs this weekend, August 8 and 9, at Smash Pickleball. If you haven't been to a CNPL weekend before, the format is a fast, team-based one: a handful of clubs go head-to-head over two days, momentum swings hard from Saturday into Sunday, and titles have a habit of coming down to a single game. The league's C4 season has been tight all summer, with playoff spots already being claimed, so the Central Split carries real stakes for teams trying to lock in position. For anyone in southwestern Ontario, it's an easy, affordable way to see high-level pickleball in person.

## PPA brings its Canada 250 to Vancouver

Looking further out, two of the biggest dates on the Canadian calendar are now in view. The PPA Tour brings its Canada 250 stop to the Vancouver area from August 19 to 23, giving West Coast fans a chance to watch touring pros in person and putting Canadian results directly into the PPA's newly announced global ranking system. That worldwide leaderboard, unveiled by the tour on August 5, pulls PPA events from around the world onto a single list, which means strong showings by Canadian players now register on the same scale as results from the U.S., Europe, and Asia. It's a small but meaningful step toward Canadian talent getting measured on the world stage.

## Nationals head to the GTA

Then comes the season's centrepiece. The 2026 Pickleball Canada National Championship is set for late August in the Greater Toronto Area, hosted at Pickleplex Social Club's Downsview and Vaughan locations. It's a multi-day event spread across a bank of indoor courts, and spectator entry is free, which makes it one of the most accessible ways to catch the country's best amateurs and pros all in one place. If you've been meaning to turn a weekend of watching into inspiration for your own game, this is the one.

## Growth keeps climbing

Zoom out and the growth story underneath all of this is hard to miss. Recent survey figures put the number of Canadians playing pickleball at roughly 1.8 million, up meaningfully from the year before, with Ontario, Quebec, and British Columbia leading the way. We'd treat any single participation figure as an estimate rather than gospel, but the direction is unmistakable: more players, more leagues, and more events every season. That's exactly why weekends like the Central Split matter — they give a fast-growing recreational base something to aspire to. We'll keep tracking the CNPL playoff race, the Vancouver stop, and the national championship as August unfolds.
