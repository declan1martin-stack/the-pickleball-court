One of the best things about pro pickleball right now is how quickly the next generation is arriving. This week gave recreational fans a couple of great windows into that pipeline, and if you're the type who likes to say "I watched them before they were famous," now is the time to start paying attention.

The clearest proving ground was the Seattle Challenger, the PPA's developmental-tier event held August 14–16 at the Sideout Tsunami Pickleball Center. The Challenger circuit is where rising players sharpen their games against tour-level competition and chase the ranking points that open doors to the main draw — and increasingly, those points matter more than ever thanks to the Carvana PPA Tour's new World Pickleball Rankings. The single global leaderboard, launched earlier this month, pulls events worldwide onto one board, which means a strong week at a Challenger no longer disappears into a regional footnote. For a young player trying to climb, that visibility is a real gift.

Up at the top of the sport, the MLP playoffs in Newport Beach doubled as a showcase for youth. Team rosters this weekend were dotted with names that have been rising fast: Jorja Johnson, who has become one of the most feared women's doubles players in the league; Hayden Patriquin and Gabe Tardio, two young men making their presence felt in high-leverage lineups for the St. Louis Shock; and Jade Kawamoto, holding her own alongside veterans on the LA Mad Drops. Watching players still early in their careers trade games with established stars on a playoff stage is exactly the kind of thing that makes a Tuesday-night league match feel like it has stakes.

Because the weekend's results were still being decided as this piece published, we're not going to hand out medals that haven't been won — but the storylines are worth following on replay, and several of these names could shape the semifinal picture in New York in two weeks.

The rising-star story is also a global one. Pickleball's youth movement stretches well beyond North America now, with talented juniors and early-career players emerging across Asia, Europe, and beyond as the pro pathways expand. That international breadth is precisely what the new World Pickleball Rankings are built to capture, and it hints at a future where the "next big thing" might just as easily come from Taipei or Madrid as from Florida or California.

For recreational players, the practical takeaway is simple: the sport's talent pool is getting deeper and younger, and the tools to follow it are getting better. Bookmark the Challenger results, keep an eye on which young players are earning playoff minutes, and enjoy the fact that today's underdogs are tomorrow's headliners. We'll keep this Rising Stars watch going each week and flag the breakout performances worth your time.
