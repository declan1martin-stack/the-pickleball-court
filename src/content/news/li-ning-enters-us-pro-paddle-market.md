More brands are chasing the same high-intent paddle buyers. In early 2026, **Li-Ning** — long known in badminton, basketball, and broader sporting goods — launched **two paddles** aimed at competitive U.S. players, according to industry tracking coverage. The move fits a wider wave of new-brand and crossover athletic brands testing the pro paddle market this year.

## Why another brand launch matters (and why it might not)

Shelf space and ad budgets are finite. Every new “pro” SKU adds comparison noise: similar carbon faces, similar thickness options, and similar influencer seeding. Li-Ning's debut does not, by itself, change which paddle you should buy tomorrow. It does mean shoppers comparing performance paddles in 2026 will see more logos competing for the same “tournament-ready” positioning.

For recreational and intermediate players, brand pedigree in another racket sport is a weak buying criterion. Grip size, weight, balance, thickness, and face texture still do the real work.
