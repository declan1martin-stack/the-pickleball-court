Full-foam paddles are no longer niche experiments. Engage's **X2** is a second-generation take aimed at power and spin without a traditional honeycomb polymer core — worth watching if you are comparing Gen 4 foam options against polymer-core flagships.

Engage previously shipped **Profoam** in 2025; the X2 follows with a **Quad Density** foam-core story in brand and review coverage. Pro **Eric Oncins** switching to the X2 in early 2026 and appearing in the top 10 in men's and mixed doubles on the PPA Tour is a competitive signal, not a guarantee that the same paddle will suit a 3.5 recreational player. Pros get custom setups, unlimited practice time, and sponsorship incentives that club players do not.

## What foam-core marketing usually means for shoppers

“Full-foam” and multi-density language usually points at dwell, plow-through, and durability claims versus classic polymer honeycomb. It does **not** automatically mean more forgiveness, a larger sweet spot, or a better buy than a well-matched carbon face with a conventional core. For a plain-English walkthrough of those marketing terms, read our [foam cores explainer](/guides/pickleball-paddle-foam-cores-explained).

1. Name the job: power/spin flagship vs. control-first all-court paddle.
2. Confirm thickness, shape, and grip size on the actual listing you will buy — model names get reused across years.
