The biggest week on the Canadian pickleball calendar is nearly here. The 2026 IG Wealth Management National Championships, presented by Hearing Life, run August 24–30 in the Greater Toronto Area, and whether you're competing, cheering on a friend, or just curious what national-level play looks like up close, it's shaping up to be a spectacular week. Here's a friendly rundown to help you get the most out of it.

Start with the setup. Pickleball Canada tapped Pickleplex Social Club as host, spreading competition across two purpose-built facilities at its Downsview and Vaughan locations. Together they offer 21 indoor courts, which means matches run efficiently and the play stays comfortable regardless of what late-summer weather does outside. Organizers actually added an extra day to this year's schedule to keep up with demand, expanding the field to as many as 1,000 participants across six days of competition. That's a lot of pickleball.

The format covers the full spread of events: women's and men's singles, women's and men's doubles, and mixed doubles, plus Pickleball Canada's Provincial Team Competition (PTC), which runs during the championship week. The PTC is always one of the most fun storylines to follow, as provinces send their best to battle for national bragging rights — think of it as the team-pride centerpiece bolted onto an already deep individual bracket. With skill levels spanning 3.0 all the way to 5.0+, you'll see everything from sharp recreational competition to genuinely elite rallies, sometimes on courts just steps apart.

For spectators, the value proposition is hard to beat: entry is free. If you're anywhere near the GTA next week, you can wander in, watch top Canadian talent, and pick up more tactical ideas in an afternoon than a month of YouTube tutorials would give you. Watching live is also the best way to appreciate the pace and spin of higher-level doubles — the resets, the speed-ups, the patience in the kitchen — in a way broadcasts don't always capture.

A few practical tips if you're planning to go. Give yourself time to explore both venues if you can, since divisions are split across the two sites. Bring water and comfortable shoes, because you'll likely do more walking (and standing courtside) than you expect. And if you're a player between matches, the event is a golden networking opportunity — Nationals tends to draw clubs and coaches from across the country.

Zooming out, this event is a milestone for a sport that keeps surging in Canada, where an estimated 1.80 million people now play. A national championship that needs 21 indoor courts and caps at 1,000 competitors simply wouldn't have been possible a few years ago. Nationals is where that growth becomes tangible — a full week of high-level competition, provincial pride, and community energy under one roof. We'll be following along all week and reporting back on the standout moments.
