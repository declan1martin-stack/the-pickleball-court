It finally happened for the New Jersey 5s. After back-to-back runner-up finishes, the top seed broke through to win their first-ever Major League Pickleball championship, sweeping the St. Louis Shock 2-0 in front of a packed Central Park crowd at CityPickle at Wollman Rink. Fittingly for a season this unpredictable, both title matches came down to a Dreambreaker — New Jersey edging a nerve-shredding 22-20 on Saturday before pulling away 21-9 on Sunday. Federico Staksrud was named Finals MVP, capping a dominant weekend for a roster that also features the sport's biggest star, Anna Leigh Waters.

It was a fitting end to a playoff run full of chaos. Remember, two lower seeds — Dallas and Brooklyn — crashed the semifinals by knocking off higher-ranked teams, including a lineup with Ben Johns. In the end, though, the two top seeds met for the crown, and the 5s proved that depth plus a superstar is a very hard combination to beat. For a franchise that had come agonizingly close twice before, this one clearly meant a lot.

If you missed it live, the finale aired on CBS on Sunday — a genuine network window for pro pickleball, and exactly the kind of exposure the sport has been chasing. The team format, with its mixed-gender lineups and sudden-death Dreambreakers, translates beautifully to a broad audience, and a Central Park backdrop didn't hurt.

Here's the wild part: there's no offseason breather. The very next day, the pro tour flipped straight into its season-opening major. The Veolia Pickleball National Championships tipped off August 31 in Cary, North Carolina, and run through September 6. This one carries a "Slam" designation — the largest prize money and ranking-point haul in the sport, worth 2,000 points, with a field of more than 1,400 players and the new World Pickleball Rankings in effect for a major for the first time.

The draw is stacked. World No. 1 Anna Leigh Waters is entered in singles, doubles, and mixed — chasing yet another triple crown to add to a résumé that already includes a staggering number of gold medals since she turned pro at age 12. Ben Johns headlines the men's doubles field, Gabe Tardio is a mixed contender, and Chris Haworth is in the singles mix. There's a hometown angle, too: North Carolina residents including world No. 2 Kate Fahey and world No. 4 Brooke Buckner will have plenty of local support.

With a fresh MLP champion just crowned and a 2,000-point major immediately on the board, the World Pickleball Rankings are about to move in a big way. It's the busiest, highest-stakes stretch of the calendar, and a perfect on-ramp if you're just getting into watching the pros.
